# Hurdles

- MODIS CSVs name their columns `brightness` and `bright_t31`, not `bright_ti4` and `bright_ti5`, and archive (SP) files add a `type` column: the parser reads only the columns it needs, by name.
- `acq_time` has no leading zeros (`6` is 00:06 UTC, `508` is 05:08): it is read as a number and split into hours and minutes.
- FIRMS has no NOAA-21 archive (SP) source: the replay uses NOAA-20, Suomi NPP and MODIS, and the manifest records NOAA-21 SP as missing.
- httpx logs every request URL at INFO, and the FIRMS MAP_KEY sits in the URL: a log filter masks it as `***`, and every error is masked before it is logged, stored or raised.
- FIRMS answers a bad key or an exceeded limit with HTTP 200 and plain text: an answer counts only if it starts with the CSV header.
- Many CWFIS hotspots are FIRMS detections republished. A CWFIS row within 50 m of a FIRMS detection with the same FRP is merged into that twin; otherwise a CWFIS hotspot from the same satellite within 1 km and 30 minutes is merged. In the replay's 24 hours that is 148 merges (128 twins, 20 same-satellite), not the 225 first reported.
- The first merge rule (1 km and 30 minutes, any satellite) joined passes of different satellites: 101 of its 225 merges paired two satellites (e.g. NOAA-21 with Suomi NPP). A merge now needs the same satellite; detections from different satellites are independent observations and both count.
- Git converts line endings on Windows checkouts: `.gitattributes` keeps the raw FIRMS CSVs byte for byte.
- `lastSeen` was already a time string on each fire: it became the object with the time, satellite and latency class, and `lastSeen.time` holds the old value.
- Merging decides which detections make up the Long Lake fire, so its centre moves slightly and a shown distance can change by 1 km (Halifax 127 → 128 km; Moncton 159 → 158 → 159 km): the view tests use the rebuilt demo values; no verdict changed.
- CWFIS gives only a report time (`rep_date`; the layer has no observation-time field), and it can be hours late: the 9 `NASA_w` rows at Long Lake reported at 07:00 UTC, with no satellite named, are FIRMS's Terra detections from 00:06 UTC (same spots within 7 m, same FRP). They now merge into their FIRMS twins and take the acquisition time and satellite. A report time counts toward the 24-hour window but never as when a satellite saw a fire, so Long Lake reads “Terra saw it burning 10 hours ago” (01:43 UTC), not “in the last 5 hours”, and the badge names Terra.
- A fire with no satellite observation says it is on Canada's official active fire list only when it has a CWFIS fire record.
- ECCC's documentation names the FireWork smoke layer `RAQDPS-FW.SFC_PM2.5-DIFF`; MSC GeoMet answers “Layer not available” to it. The layer GeoMet lists today is `RAQDPS.Sfc_PM2.5-WildfireSmokePlume`, found in its full capabilities (40 MB); the engine names it in one place.
- GeoMet answers its own errors (a layer it does not have, an hour past the run's last) with HTTP 200 and XML, and a point outside the model with `{}`: an answer counts only if it is the JSON for the hour asked, with one value.
- GeoMet gives one hour per request and refuses a range or a list of times: 48 hours are 48 requests, asked 6 at a time (about a second in all), then kept an hour for the 0.1° cell.
- A model run published while the 48 hours are being asked would mix two forecasts: the first hour's answer names the run, and the other 47 are asked for that run by name.
- The layer's value is in kg/m³ and ECCC's legend in µg/m³; ECCC's answer also names the legend class. The engine takes the class and checks the value sits in it, so a change of units upstream gives “not available”, not a wrong bar.
- ECCC keeps a smoke forecast about two days (GeoMet), about seven weeks on its long archive: none exists for the replay of Aug 25, 2025, which says “forecast not available”. `scripts/fetch_smoke_forecast.py` records one on the day for a future replay.
- The first “Best time to air out” tile was larger than the Call 911 button on the verdict that nothing explains, where Call 911 must be the largest thing to tap; the existing test for that caught it. On that verdict the tile now waits behind “Why?”.
- On a phone zoomed to 200% (195 px wide) the top bar's Back, Listen and EN/FR do not fit side by side and were cut off: on the air-out screen they go one under the other below 300 px. The other screens still need it.
- The two home-screen tests that open a second, full Chromium (`keep.spec.ts`, “in full Chromium”) failed in the full browser run and once in five tries each alone, with a 90-second timeout or a 30-second timeout on closing the browser. The cause is memory, not the tests and not the processor: with eight checkouts open and running their suites, the 7.7 GB machine had 54 to 170 MB of RAM available, 26.9 GB committed and 22,000 to 44,000 pages a second going to and from the page file. During a stall the browser's processes held 0 to 9 MB in RAM of the 40 to 70 MB they own, used almost no processor time and took a few hundred page faults a second: they were being paged back in, for 30 to 100 seconds, at whatever step came next (loading the page, asking Chrome if the site is installable, closing). The same steps against the deployed site stalled the same way; the lighter headless shell the other tests use stalled in 1 try of 11. Rerun once 540 MB or more of RAM was free, each test passed 5 times in 5 alone, in 1.5 to 2.9 seconds, and the same steps ran 6 times in 6 with the processor still at 90 to 99%: flakes of a machine short of memory, not of a busy processor. Run these two tests with memory to spare.
- A sentence that ends on “a.m.” took a second period (“until Sun 8 a.m..”): the screen's sentences drop it.
- Building a date formatter for each of the 48 hours made the forecast screen take seconds to draw: one formatter, made once.
- The region's newest FIRMS detection in the replay (NOAA-20, 06:31 UTC) is in Massachusetts, not at Long Lake: the badge only ever uses the fire's own newest detection, never a region-wide one.
- The first live check after a quiet spell ended on "no data" while a check seconds later passed: Render spins a free service down after 15 idle minutes and wipes its disk, so a wake-up starts the engine with no saved wind grid, and for about two minutes every live verdict was a 503 `wind_data_unavailable`, which the app took for a real no-data answer. Now the engine says `"status": "warming"` with `Retry-After` while that first grid loads, the app wakes the engine with one `/health` request as it opens in live mode, and Loading keeps trying for up to three minutes, a whole cold start, saying so, keeping "no data" for the engine’s own answer.
- MapLibre GL JS 6 ships as three ES modules, and its worker imports the same shared module as the page: bundled into the app, the worker would carry a second copy (about 450 kB gzip to download instead of 306 kB). The three are served as they are from `/vendor/`, and loaded from there.
- Map names need letters. A glyph server would be one more host: with no "glyphs" address in the style, MapLibre 6 draws each letter itself with a font the page has. It reads the weight out of the font's name, so the style asks for "Inter Semibold", then "Inter".
- With no red cones the navy ribbon and the orange embers come under 3:1 against each other (2.89). The ribbon carries a white casing, which holds 3:1 or more against the embers for every kind of colour blindness checked.
- Long Lake has no record on Canada's official fire list in the recorded data: on the map it is its 456 detections and its name, never a flame. A flame is drawn only for a fire with a record.
- `pmtiles extract` gives the same bytes every time for the same build, box and zooms: the basemap is pinned by its SHA-256. Protomaps keeps the first build of each tile schema version, so that is the build pinned.
- ECCC's zone outline is asked for in a second query, only when an alert is active: the alert check keeps its own query, its 5 seconds and its "not checked" rules, untouched.
- The sheet's handle takes 56 px that the old screen did not need. On the smallest phones the sheet at half stands over the top bar, so the three badges and "Why?" still show above the 911 bar without a scroll.
- The map's buttons and the two marks compete for a small map. The frame is the closest view that leaves both marks whole and under no button; where there is none, the zoom buttons and then the credit give way (the credit is always in the legend).
- The copyright sign of "© OpenStreetMap" is in Unicode's pictographic set, and MapLibre's web worker is not a service worker: the two guards that caught them now tell the difference.
- MapLibre's `maxBounds` keeps the whole picture inside its bounds, the part hidden under the sheet too. With no fire in range the frame sits low on the map, and MapLibre pushed it down under the sheet, without a word. The map now holds only its centre near the file's box (`transformConstrain`). Found by the independent review, by computing; no browser test had opened that state on the detailed map. One does now.
- Taking a MapLibre map down gives its WebGL context back on purpose, which fires "context lost": a listener that reads that as "this device cannot draw the map" blamed the device for a failed tile. The listener now knows when the map was taken down.
- `isStyleLoaded()` is false whenever a tile is loading, and "load" fires once in a map's life: a language change made at that moment was dropped. The names are now set as soon as the style is in, and again on every change.
- The detailed map's canvas covered the outline map from the moment it was created, so the person saw plain sea, then tiles popping in. It now stays out of sight until its first picture is whole, and it is started during the Loading screen so that picture is ready early.
- "No satellite saw fire within 500 km" was said even when NASA FIRMS had not answered. What the map says in words now follows the app's rule: "not checked" instead of a guess, in the summary and in Listen, not only in the legend.
- With only the card showing, a tall sheet (a fire close by, French, a small phone, 200% zoom) stood over the top bar and made New check and EN/FR unreachable, with no button to lower it. At that height the sheet now stops under the bar and scrolls inside.
- Two history steps for one double tap: the sheet and the legend live in the browser's history so Back closes them, and a second press before the first had landed went back twice, out of the verdict. A change is now asked once per history entry.
- A headless browser draws WebGL in software, many times slower than a phone's graphics chip, and this machine (7.7 GB of memory, shared with other test runs) was at its limit all night: the map's timings are reported with both, and read with that in mind.
- New Brunswick's burn category layer dates each category `VALIDDATE`, which reads `14:00Z` and is neither UTC nor the time of the update: it is 2 p.m. Atlantic, the end of validity, stored as wall-clock time. The server's own clock (`VALIDDATE > CURRENT_TIMESTAMP + INTERVAL '11' HOUR`) and a sister layer's noon readings stamped `13:00Z` settled it.
- ArcGIS Server sends its errors with HTTP 200 and a body of `{"error": …}`: one is a failure, and is never kept as the province's answer.
- The province's county outlines follow the shore, and a town's listed point can sit in its harbour (Bathurst's is 170 m out): a point in no county gets the only county within 250 m, away from every border.
- The same outlines take in six islets of Washington County, Maine (Treat Island, Dudley Island, St. Croix Island and ledges): left out at build time.
- The map's coarse outline takes Calais, Maine for New Brunswick: for the burn card, a point in none of the province's counties is outside the province, whatever the map says.
- The fire season is not a calendar: 2026's opened on April 1 by the Minister's order, three weeks before the third Monday of April. A category the province gives is shown whatever the date.
- Two Listen buttons on one screen talked over each other, and one leaving the screen cut the other's sentence: one voice at a time, and only the button that is reading can stop the voice.
- A card computed once from the check's time kept saying "valid until 2 p.m. tomorrow" on a screen left open for days: it follows the phone's clock, never one that runs behind the check.
