# 0003: The verdict's map: its basemap, its overlay, and what happens when it cannot be drawn

- Status: accepted, Oct 4, 2026 (feature 3a, "the answer lives on the map")
- Decided by: the overnight lane on `feat/map`, alone; each choice is open to the owner's review
- Code: `web/src/map/`, `web/src/screens/Verdict.tsx`, `engine/smoke_engine/map.py`

## Context

The answer this app gives is about a place: the air you are breathing came from over there, and it passed that
fire. Until now the picture of it was a small static map of province outlines, behind "Why?". Feature 3a makes
the map the screen the verdict opens on, with the answer card in a sheet at its foot.

What the map has to show: the air's path at three heights with a mark every hour (the ribbon), the satellite
fire detections sized by their fire power, the fires on Canada's official list, the person, and ECCC's forecast
zone when an air-quality alert is active. Each with its source and its time.

What bounds the choice (the North Star, and the lane's bar):

- A worried 80-year-old must read it in five seconds. Buttons for every action, words 18 px or more, calm.
- ECCC must be able to trust it: every layer sourced, "not checked" instead of a guess, no claim past the data.
- No one else learns where a person is. No account, no key, nothing to pay per view.
- The map must never cost the answer: if it cannot be drawn, the verdict is still whole.
- The first screens must not get heavier; the map itself at most 350 kB gzipped.
- English and French as equals. Our own look, never a Government of Canada one.

## Decision 1. The basemap: our own PMTiles file of OpenStreetMap, drawn by MapLibre GL JS

| Option | Why not, or why |
|---|---|
| A hosted tile service (a commercial one, or OpenStreetMap's own servers) | A key and a bill or a usage policy; every map view tells a third party roughly where the person is; one more service that can be down |
| Raster tiles we host | Thousands of files; a fixed look (green forests, English-only names) we cannot restyle; blurred between zooms |
| Keep only the bundled outlines | No towns, no roads: enough to fall back on, too little for the place the answer lives |
| **One PMTiles file of vector tiles, on the same static host** | One file, read by byte range; restyled in our colours; names in the app's language; nothing to run |

What was chosen, exactly:

- **The file**: Protomaps' build of OpenStreetMap, cut to the engine's wind grid (`-72,41,-56,51`), tile zooms 0
  to 10: 2,645 tiles, 46.2 MB. Zoom 10 shows a town and its roads; the map lets a person go two zooms closer,
  where the same tiles are enlarged.
- **Pinned**: build `20260928` (the first build of tile schema 4.15.2; Protomaps keeps the first build of each
  schema version, and build `20251020` still answers), cut by go-pmtiles 1.31.2 (its archive checked against a
  SHA-256 for each platform), and the result checked against its own size and SHA-256. Two cuts gave the same
  bytes. `npm run tiles` fetches it; it is never committed; `npm run deploy` fetches it first.
- **The library**: MapLibre GL JS 6.12.0. It ships as three ES modules, and its worker imports the same shared
  module as the page. Bundled into the app, the worker would get a copy of its own: about 450 kB gzip to
  download instead of 306 kB. So the three are copied to `/vendor/maplibre-gl-6.12.0/` as they are and loaded
  from there.
- **The look**: land, water, built-up areas, main roads, borders, place names. No green (forests and parks are
  plain land: smoke is never "all clear"). Names in English or French from OpenStreetMap's own `name:en` and
  `name:fr`.
- **The names' font**: with no "glyphs" address in the style, MapLibre 6 draws each letter itself with a font
  the page already has. Inter at 600, 18 px: no glyph server, no font file to ship. (The page's Inter comes
  from Google Fonts, as it did before the map: the map adds no third party, and removes none.)
- **Where the map may go**: its centre is held within the file's box plus half a screen, so some of the
  Maritimes is always in view. MapLibre's own `maxBounds` was tried first and dropped: it keeps the whole
  picture inside its bounds, the part hidden under the sheet too, and pushed the opening frame down under the
  sheet when no fire was in range.

## Decision 2. The overlay: one SVG over either basemap, carried by a transform while the map moves

| Option | Why not, or why |
|---|---|
| MapLibre's own layers (GeoJSON sources) | Drawn on the canvas: nothing for a test or a screen reader to look at; a flame and labels need sprite and glyph files; and the outline map would need a second overlay of its own |
| An HTML marker for each mark | About 600 elements moved one by one on every frame |
| **One SVG, in app code, over whichever basemap is there** | One overlay for both basemaps; every mark can be found and measured by a test; drawn in the app's own type |

How it stays in step with the basemap: a single view (a scale and an offset from Web Mercator to the screen)
places everything. MapLibre is asked where it puts two known places, and the view is read from its answer, so
the overlay sits on the basemap by construction. While a finger moves the map, the overlay's wrapper gets a CSS
transform and nothing inside it is redrawn. When the map rests, the overlay is drawn once through the new view.
It is drawn 256 px past each edge of the map, so a drag that far shows nothing missing.

What follows from this: during a pinch the marks grow and shrink with the map until it rests (as in most web
maps); labels sit on small white plates so they stay readable over the basemap's own names.

## Decision 3. When the detailed map cannot be drawn: the outline map, there from the first moment

The outline map (bundled outlines, d3-geo) is drawn at once. The detailed map is started while the Loading
screen still shows, off the screen, on the frame the verdict is expected to open on: its tiles are fetched and
its first picture drawn before the verdict shows, and the verdict screen takes it as it is. It stays out of
sight and out of reach until that first picture is drawn; then it takes the outline map's place. If it cannot,
the outline map simply stays, with the same overlay, and a note says why in plain words:

| What happened | What the person sees |
|---|---|
| The tiles, or the map library, did not load (also when a host answers the tiles' address with a web page, and when nothing has drawn after 20 seconds on a network that neither answers nor fails) | "Simple map: the detailed map could not be loaded. It shows the same things." |
| The browser has no WebGL 2, or only a very slow one | "Simple map: this device cannot draw the detailed map. It shows the same things." |
| The engine sent no `map` key, a null, or a version the app does not know | The paths, the person and the fire's name, and: "Simple map: the fire detections and the alert zone are not on it right now. The air's path is." The legend says "not on the map" for each missing layer, never a number |

The + and − buttons and Recentre work on the outline map too.

Two more cases, both rare. If the browser takes the map's picture back (a tab left in the background), the
outline map shows until MapLibre has drawn again: no note, nothing lost. If the map's own code breaks while it
is drawn, the answer, the sheet and Call 911 stay as they are and the map's place says: "The map cannot be
shown right now. The answer below does not depend on it."

## Decision 4. The sheet and the buttons

- The sheet has three heights, each as tall as what it holds: the card (peek); the card, the three badges and
  "Why?" (half); everything "Why?" says (full). The screen opens at peek: the card is the answer, and the map
  gets the room.
- Every move has a button with words: "Sources and why", "Show the map", "Why?". A flick on the handle and the
  arrow keys do the same. Back lowers the sheet and closes the legend, and coming back from another screen
  finds the sheet as it was left. Two presses at once count as one.
- With only the card showing, the sheet never stands over the top bar: New check and EN/FR stay in reach, and a
  card taller than the room scrolls in it. Raised, the sheet may stand over the bar (always with "Why?" open);
  one press lowers it. On a screen with no room for a map at all (a small window at 200% zoom), the button that
  lowers the sheet says "Show less", not "Show the map".
- The map has + and − buttons, Legend, and Recentre (shown once the map has been moved; it calls no GPS).
  Dragging the map is an extra, and the arrow keys pan it.
- The map opens on the person and the fire, both whole above the sheet and under no button. On a map too small
  for everything, the buttons give way to the marks: first the zoom buttons, then the credit (which is always
  in the legend), and only where that lets the marks show. With no fire in range the frame holds the path the
  verdict was read from; where even the farthest zoom cannot hold it all, the person stays in view and the
  path's far end runs off the edge.
- While the sheet or the legend covers the whole map, the map does nothing: no new frame, no tiles fetched.
- Call 911 stays at the foot of the screen at every height, and with the legend open.

## Decision 5. The engine's `map` key

A new key in `GET /verdict`, version 1, with a strict JSON Schema
(`engine/smoke_engine/schemas/map.v1.schema.json`) that both sides are tested against. It is built after the
verdict from what the verdict already used, by one function for live and replay. The schema requires every
layer to name its source and its time; null means not known. A test holds that the answer with and without the
key is the same but for the key.

ECCC's zone outline is asked for in a second query, only when an alert is active, with 5 seconds of its own,
and the answer is kept 15 minutes for its point (checks from one town under one alert ask once). The alert
check itself (its query, its timeout, its "not checked" rules) is as it was. The outline is simplified to
about 500 m and to 600 points at most; one that is still larger is not sent, and the map says the zone could
not be drawn. The simplifier is tested on a real outline recorded from ECCC's API on Oct 4, 2026 (801 points
in, 37 out, about 10 ms).

What the map says in words follows one rule: nothing is said to be absent when its source was not checked.
The summary (the screen reader's, Listen's, and the legend's first lines) names each source that did not
answer, and says "could not be checked" when none did.

## Decision 6. No pan buttons

- Decided by: the owner, Oct 4, 2026, as the map was merged into main

The map gets no buttons to move it sideways: no pad of four arrows, no "go to the fire", no "go to the person".
Every fact the map shows is in the sheet as text, and the map is supplementary: nothing a person needs depends
on moving it.

The map keeps the buttons it has: Legend, + and −, and Recentre. A drag and the arrow keys still move it, as
extras. What this leaves open is named under "To live with": the drag has no single-pointer alternative.

## Consequences

**Good**

- Nothing to run and nothing to pay: one static file. No third party learns which part of the map a person
  looks at: the tiles are the app's own file.
- The first screens are lighter than before, because the verdict screen is now a file of its own: the main
  bundle is 168,331 bytes gzip (175,860 before the map). The map's own download is 316,539 bytes gzip
  (MapLibre 306,349, the app's map code 10,190), under the 350 kB set for it.
- The map is on the screen 0.7 to 1.2 s after the verdict on a slowed-down phone profile (processor 4 times
  slower, 1.6 Mbit/s), when the machine measuring it is not saturated; two runs of six on a saturated machine
  missed the 1.5 s set for it (1.9 and 2.2 s). The six runs are in PLAN-NOTES.md. What made the difference:
  the library starts coming while the person picks their place, and the map is started during the Loading
  screen.
- The same bytes every time: the basemap can be rebuilt by anyone from its pin.
- Every mark on the map can be tested, and is: where it is, what it is, that it does not move without the map.

**To live with**

- The 46 MB file is not in git. A build without it works and shows the outline map; `npm run deploy` fetches
  it first, and the browser tests fetch it too.
- The host must answer byte ranges (HTTP 206). The local server does; Vercel's static hosting does for the
  files the live site already serves. The extract itself has not been deployed from this lane (the lane is
  local-only): one command in the report confirms it after the next deploy.
- MapLibre 6 needs WebGL 2. A phone without it gets the outline map.
- Moving the map freely has no button: Recentre, + and − are buttons, and the arrow keys pan. A drag is not
  one of the gestures WCAG 2.1 asks a button for (2.5.1 covers multipoint and path-based gestures); WCAG 2.2's
  2.5.7 does ask for one. Buttons that go to the fire and to the person, or a small pad of four arrows, would
  close this. The owner decided against them (Decision 6): every fact is in the sheet as text, the map is
  supplementary, and every button on it competes with the ribbon.
- On a map too small for them, the zoom buttons give way; the map then zooms with two fingers or the keyboard
  only. The outline map does not pan.
- A fire is drawn as a flame only when it is on Canada's official list. Long Lake has no record there in the
  recorded data, so it is its 456 detections and its name, not a flame.
- The small static map behind "Why?" is still there, with the fire's smoke traced forward. It goes when 3b
  puts that smoke on the main map.
- When an alert is active, the engine makes one more request to ECCC (once per place in 15 minutes).
- On the busiest fire days the overlay redraws up to 4,000 dots each time the map comes to rest. They are
  drawn only where they are in or near the frame; a canvas layer for the dots is the next step if that is felt.
- A missing tiles, vendor or assets file is "not found" on the host, never the app's own page (which the
  browser would otherwise keep for a year under the rule that caches those folders for good). Not yet checked
  on the host itself: that a "not found" answer does not carry that rule too. The check to run after the next
  deploy is in the lane's report.
- The built files (`/assets/`) are now kept by the browser for good, like the tiles and the map library: their
  names change when their contents do.
- The screens' own browser tests run without WebGL (on the outline map); the detailed map has its own test
  project.

## Reviewed

A fresh reviewer read the whole change against four questions: is Call 911 reachable at every height, does the
map claim more than the data, is it fast, is it accessible. It found no fault with Call 911, nor with the
map's separation from the verdict. It found fourteen defects elsewhere; each is fixed and held by a test
(`web/e2e/map.spec.ts`, "found in review"; `web/src/map/*.test.ts`; `engine/tests/test_map.py`). The ones
that changed this record: the opening frame with no fire in range (`maxBounds`, above), the top bar going out
of reach with only the card showing, "No satellite saw fire" said when NASA FIRMS had not been checked, the
detailed map covering the outline map before it had drawn, and a failed tile blamed on the device.
