# 0005. "Is burning allowed today?": the province's burn status on the verdict screen

Date: 2026-10-04. Status: accepted, on branch `feat/burn`.

## Context

On Aug 25, 2025, Moncton Fire's deputy chief asked people to stop backyard fires, and said his crews had been called
to mulch fires. A person who has just checked the smoke is the right person to tell, at that moment, whether burning
is allowed where they live. The answer exists: the Government of New Brunswick gives each county a burn category every
day at 2 p.m. during the fire season. It is on a map most people never open.

## Decision

A card on the verdict screen, under "Why?", says for the person's county whether burning is allowed today, from the
province's own data, or says "not checked". It is shown in New Brunswick only. It never changes the verdict.

Live sample, 2026-10-04 05:41 UTC (2:41 a.m. Atlantic), `…/MapServer/0/query?where=1=1&outFields=NAME,PUBLICCATEGORY,VALIDDATE&returnGeometry=false&f=json`:
all 15 counties `PUBLICCATEGORY` 3, `VALIDDATE` 1791122400000. Layer 1: no rows. Asked again by the engine itself at
11:18 UTC: the same rows, answered in 0.28 s; Moncton read `permitted`, Westmorland, valid until 2026-10-04T17:00:00Z.

**What `VALIDDATE` is.** 1791122400000 reads `2026-10-04T14:00:00Z`, but it is 2 p.m. Atlantic, the end of the
category's validity (the next update), stored as wall-clock time and sent as if it were UTC. Three things say so:
it was in the future at 2:41 a.m. on Oct 4, so it is not the time of the update; the server's own clock put it between
11 and 12 hours ahead of its "now" (`VALIDDATE > CURRENT_TIMESTAMP + INTERVAL '11' HOUR` matched 15 counties, `'12'`
matched none), which is 2 p.m. minus 2:49 a.m. and not 14:00 UTC minus 05:49 UTC; and the sister layer
`DailyFireWeather` stamps its readings, taken at noon standard time, `13:00Z`. The reviewer repeated the clock test
at 11:34 UTC with the same result. The engine converts it with the Atlantic clock rule (daylight time from the second
Sunday of March to the first Sunday of November).

## Rules, each one tested (`engine/tests/test_burn.py`, `web/src/burn/view.test.ts`, `web/e2e/burn.spec.ts`)

1. **The county is the one whose outline holds the point.** The outlines are the province's own, from the same
   layers, downloaded once (`engine/scripts/build_counties.py`, `data/places/nb-counties.geojson`, generalised by the
   province's server to about 5 m). The person's point is never sent to the province: the engine asks for the list of
   all 15 counties, at most once in 15 minutes.
2. **Outside New Brunswick the card is hidden** (`burn` is null). A point in none of the province's counties is
   outside, whatever the app's coarser map says (it takes Calais, Maine for New Brunswick). The one exception is a
   New Brunswick town's own listed point, which reads "not checked".
3. **A category is shown only as the province names it.** Code 1, 2 or 3, and only while the layer's own coded value
   for that code reads word for word as it did, in both languages. Swapped codes, other hours, added words:
   not checked.
4. **Over 26 hours old: not checked.** A category is a day old at `VALIDDATE`, so it is shown until `VALIDDATE` plus
   2 hours, by the engine and again by the card on a screen left open. One dated more than 36 hours ahead is not a
   day's status: not checked.
5. **The fire season is the province's to open and close.** A category the province gives is shown whatever the date.
   "Fire season closed" is said only when the province lists the county under "No Current Category" from November to
   March. From April to October a county with no category is not checked. (See decision 1.)
6. **Any failure: not checked.** The province not answering within 5 seconds in all, an error sent with HTTP 200
   (never kept as an answer), a cut-off list, a county missing or listed twice, a category or date of another type.
   Never a guess.
7. **It never changes the verdict** (`test_the_burn_status_never_changes_the_verdict`): the answer is the same, field
   for field, whatever the province says or fails to say.
8. **The replay is not checked**: no archived official status exists (see History). The county is still named.

## Decisions made alone

1. **The season rule departs from the brief.** The brief said: outside the fire season (after Oct 31, before the
   third Monday of April), "fire season closed" if the source says so, else not checked. The province's own dashboard
   says 2026's season ran "from Wednesday April 1st to October 31st", and its season page says the season "may be
   extended if conditions require". Under the brief's rule a real "No burn" on April 5, 2026, or in an extended
   November, would have been hidden as "not checked", and a county with no category on April 5 would have read
   "fire season closed" in an open season. So: the province's category wins over the calendar, and "closed" is said
   only from November to March, when no season has been open. To go back to the brief's rule, restore the calendar
   test at the end of `_read` in `engine/smoke_engine/burn.py`.
2. **The point stays with us.** The layer could answer "which county holds this point" itself. Asking for the whole
   list instead keeps the person's location away from a third party, and costs the province 3 small requests per
   15 minutes however many people check. The first check of each 15 minutes waits for the province, 5 seconds at most
   for its three answers together; while it does not answer it is not asked again for 2 minutes.
3. **Four guards against the wrong county.**
   - The names database (NRCan CGNDB) lists a county for each community: an independent check. Of 2,277 New Brunswick
     communities, 2,256 fall in the outline of the county it lists. For a town picked from the search (its own listed
     point), a county that differs from the outline's is not checked: 11 places, most within a few hundred metres of
     a county line where the listed point is rounded; Belledune (a village) and Carleton North (a town) are the
     largest.
   - A phone's location can be off by a kilometre. A point within 1 km of a county whose answer is another one is
     not checked. Where both counties have the same answer it is shown.
   - A phone's location the phone itself rates worse than 1 km is not used to tell a county: the card reads "not
     checked" and names none.
   - The card names the county first, so the person can see which one was used.
4. **Six islets left out of the province's outline.** The layer's Charlotte County takes in Treat Island, Dudley
   Island, St. Croix Island and three ledges, which are in Washington County, Maine (OpenStreetMap). Nobody lives on
   them. They are the only change made to the province's outlines; `build_counties.py` lists them.
5. **A town's point in its harbour.** The outlines follow the shore, and 10 listed points sit in the water (Bathurst's
   is 170 m out). A point in no county gets the only county within 250 m, but never within 15 km of Maine, Quebec,
   Nova Scotia or Prince Edward Island, where a point off the outline may be abroad.
6. **"Valid until", not "updated at".** The source carries no update time, only the end of validity. The card says
   "Valid until 2 p.m. today" in plain words. Before the day's update it adds "For this evening, check again after
   2 p.m.": tonight's category is not known yet. After it: "The province updates them every day at 2 p.m." Past the
   end of validity: "Today's update is due." The time the engine asked is under Sources.
7. **"Not checked" says why, in words true of the reason:** the province's conditions not available; near a county
   line where the two counties differ; the county not known. Each ends "That does not mean burning is allowed", and
   so does "Fire season closed", which adds whom the province says to call out of season (Environment and Local
   Government, 506-453-2690, from the dashboard's own text).
8. **Green, here only.** DESIGN-LOCK says no green: smoke is never "all clear". The brief asks for red, yellow and
   green, the province's own colours. Green is used on this card only, as the outline and shape of "Burning
   permitted", never as a fill behind text and never about the smoke. The shape holds a flame, not a check mark:
   under a verdict that says to look outside, a tick could read as "all is well". Each state has its own shape and
   word: octagon and cross, triangle and clock, circle and flame, outlined square (season closed), dashed ring (not
   checked, as on the source badges).
9. **What "permitted" means is said as the province says it:** campfires and small wood fires (its Category 1);
   "Bigger fires need a permit."
10. **Under "Why?", not above it.** The answer about the smoke stays first and nothing above the card moves: the
    small phone layouts and their tests are unchanged. The cost: on most phones the card needs a scroll.
11. **Its own Listen.** The screen's Listen reads the verdict; the card has a second button, named for a screen
    reader "Listen: is burning allowed today?". `ListenButton` now allows one voice at a time: starting one stops the
    other, and a button that is not reading never cuts the other's sentence.
12. **The town's rules link is a web search** for the town's open air burning by-law ("arrêté" in French). No single
    official list of municipal by-laws exists. The province's own advice ("check with your municipality") is linked
    under Sources.
13. **"Keep mulch damp" has a third source.** The article reports that mulch can catch fire by itself and that a
    flicked butt set mulch alight; GNB's prevention tips cover cigarette butts and backyard burning. Neither says to
    keep mulch damp. Halifax Regional Fire and Emergency does ("Keep mulch beds as moist as possible", Aug 21, 2008),
    and is credited for that tip.
14. **All text on the card is 18 px or more**, hosts under links included (elsewhere they are 16 px).

## The independent review

A fresh reviewer, given the code and not the reasoning, tried to break the wrong-county guards, the dates and the
wording. What it found and what was done:

| Found | Done |
|---|---|
| The calendar season contradicts the province (April 1, 2026; extensions): a real "No burn" hidden, "closed" said in an open season | Decision 1 |
| A screen left open kept "valid until 2 p.m. tomorrow" for days | The card follows the phone's clock: due, then not checked after 26 hours |
| A status seen at 10 a.m. says nothing of the evening | "For this evening, check again after 2 p.m." |
| "The province allows campfires and other outdoor wood fires" overclaims | Decision 9 |
| "Season closed" could read as "fine to burn"; one "not checked" sentence was untrue for a county line or an unknown county; "Checked with the province" appeared under "Not checked" | Decision 7; the time is shown only with an answer |
| Six islets of Charlotte County's outline are in Maine | Decision 4 |
| The phone's stated accuracy was ignored | Decision 3, third guard |
| "Not checked" cards shown in Calais, Houlton, Pointe-à-la-Croix (the coarse map) | Rule 2: hidden |
| The label check read only the English half | Rule 3 |
| An ArcGIS error with HTTP 200 was kept for 15 minutes; three requests could take 15 seconds; concurrent checks each fetched | Rule 6; decision 2 |
| A green tick under "unexplained smoke" | Decision 8: a flame |
| "Dry mulch can catch fire by itself": no source says "dry" | "Mulch can catch fire by itself" |
| Opening "Why?" cut the card's sentence | Decision 11 |
| French: "état de brûlage", "règlement", a pronoun with no antecedent, a link title | "conditions de brûlage" (GNB's term), "arrêté", reworded, the page's own title |

Checked by the reviewer and found right: the point-in-polygon code, the 1 km and 250 m rules against brute force, the
border with Maine walked every 0.01°, New Brunswick's own islands, the `VALIDDATE` reading, the 26 h and 36 h edges,
that the point is never sent, and that each cited source says what is credited to it.

## Known limits

- **What the layers do after October 31 is not known yet.** If the province moves the counties to "No Current
  Category", the card reads "fire season closed" from November 1; if it leaves old categories in place, "not checked".
  Both are true. Worth a look on November 1.
- **Reaching the province from Render** was not tested (nothing was deployed). From New Brunswick the service answers
  in about 0.3 s. If it cannot be reached, the card reads "not checked".
- For up to 15 minutes after the 2 p.m. update the card can show the earlier category, marked "Today's update is due".
- The province's status is not the whole rule. The card says so in one line; it cannot know a town's by-law.
- A desktop browser's location can be tens of kilometres off and still claim to be accurate: the county named on the
  card is then the check the person has.
- A New Brunswick shore point just off the outline, within 15 km of Maine, Quebec, Nova Scotia or Prince Edward
  Island (a wharf on Campobello), gets no card.
- Belledune, Carleton North and 9 smaller places read "not checked" when picked from the town search, until someone
  fixes their county in the names database or adds an exception.
