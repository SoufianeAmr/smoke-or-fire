How it works

We look at where the air around you has been over the last day, and whether it passed a fire.

  1. 1

    You tell us where you are

    Share your location, or type your town.

  2. 2

    We trace the air backward

    Using hourly wind data, we step the air arriving at your spot back in time, one hour per step.

    24 hlooked back
    24hourly steps
  3. 3

    We check for fires along the way

    At every step we measure the closest approach: the shortest distance between the air and each active fire.

    25 kmclose enough to call it
    50 kmhow far we search
  4. 4

    You get a verdict

    Drifting smoke

    The air passed within 25 km of an active fire.

    Unclear

    It passed 25 to 50 km from a fire, or the wind was unsteady.

    Unexplained smoke

    No active fire within 50 km of the air’s path.

    We add the Air Quality Health Index so you know what to do right now.

What it can’t do

A fire that just started may not show up in satellite data for hours. If you see flames or a smoke column, call 911. Don’t wait for a verdict.

Flames

Fire you can see outdoors, other than a campfire or fire pit someone is watching.

Smoke column

Smoke rising from one spot. Smoke from far away spreads out as haze; a column means the fire is close.

Dark smoke

Thick black or brown smoke. It often means a building, vehicle or dense brush is burning nearby.
